    The Case: Who Should Get the Subsidy?

    The thread that runs through every session Data: CASEN 2022 · Chile

    Every session in this course works on the same problem and the same data. Each new method answers the next question that problem raises, and nothing gets used before it has been taught.

    The problem

    Governments in emerging economies spend a large share of their social budget on programmes targeted at poor households. To target, you need to know who is poor, and that usually means income. But income is hard to observe: much of it is informal, people have reasons to under-report it, and verifying it household by household is expensive.

    The standard answer is a proxy means test: predict a household's welfare from characteristics that are easy to observe and hard to fake, such as schooling, household size, type of dwelling or health insurance. Chile's Registro Social de Hogares, Mexico's Progresa/Prospera and many World Bank–supported programmes use versions of this idea.

    That is a machine learning problem, with all of its difficulties:

    Question Where we meet it
    What kind of data do we have about a household? Session 1 · Data types
    How do we build one clean row per household? Session 2 · Data manipulation
    Can we predict income from what we observe? How wrong are we? Session 3 · ML models
    Is the error we report honest, or did we fool ourselves? Session 4 · The ML pipeline

    Can more flexible models do better, and at what cost? Session 6 · Trees and ensembles
    Which variables do we really need? Session 7 · Selection and shrinkage

    The data

    CASEN (Encuesta de Caracterización Socioeconómica Nacional) is Chile's main household survey, run by the Ministry of Social Development and Family. The 2022 round interviewed about 72,000 households. We use a random sample of 15,000 households, one row each, built from the record of the head of household.

    ⬇️ Download casen_households.csv

    import pandas as pd
url = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hizocar/machine_learning_egei/main/docs/data/casen_households.csv"
df = pd.read_csv(url)

    Data dictionary

    Column Type Description
    household_id id Course identifier (not the CASEN folio)
    region nominal Region of residence (16 regions)
    area nominal urban / rural
    head_sex nominal Sex of the head of household
    head_age continuous Age of the head of household
    head_schooling_years discrete Years of schooling of the head (missing = doesn't know)
    head_education ordinal Highest level reached, 0_none … 6_tertiary_complete
    head_activity nominal employed / unemployed / inactive
    head_job_type nominal Type of job; missing when the head does not work
    head_weekly_hours continuous Usual weekly hours; missing when the head does not work, and for 87 employed heads who did not know
    health_insurance nominal fonasa (public), isapre (private), armed_forces, none, other
    head_nationality nominal chilean / dual / foreign
    head_indigenous nominal Belongs to an indigenous people (yes / no)
    household_size discrete Number of people in the household
    has_children, has_elderly binary Anyone under 18 / over 60 in the household
    household_type nominal Single person, nuclear, extended, …
    overcrowding ordinal People per bedroom, 0_none … 3_critical
    housing_tenure nominal owned / rented / lent / irregular
    dwelling_type nominal house / apartment / precarious
    household_income continuous ⚠️ Total household income, CLP per month
    income_per_capita continuous ⚠️ Total income per person, CLP per month
    income_decile ordinal ⚠️ National income decile (1 = poorest)
    poor target 1 if the household is in income poverty
    survey_weight weight Expansion factor (how many households each row represents)

    The ⚠️ columns: target in one task, leak in another

    Income is the target when we predict income (Sessions 3–4). When we predict poverty (Session 5 onwards), income is off limits: poverty is defined from income per capita, and the whole point of a proxy means test is that income is not observed. A model that uses it scores brilliantly and is useless. Learning to spot this is part of the course.

    Two things to know about the numbers

    • Poverty measure. poor uses the updated poverty methodology published in the current CASEN 2022 database: 23% of households in our sample and about 20% of people (weighted) are poor. Under the old 2013 methodology the official 2022 figure was 6.5%.
    • Weights. CASEN is a complex survey. For official statistics you would use survey_weight. For our prediction exercises we treat each household as one example; when a result is meant to describe Chile as a whole, we say so and weight it.

    Source: Ministerio de Desarrollo Social y Familia, Encuesta CASEN 2022, current Stata database (data catalogue). The sample is rebuilt with scripts/build_casen_sample.py in the course repository.
