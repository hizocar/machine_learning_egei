    The Project

    One project, built over the second half of the course, in two deliveries, and 100% of the final grade. You do to a problem of your choice what we do in class to the CASEN case: frame it, build an honest pipeline, compare models and turn the result into a decision someone can act on. Each delivery includes the log of what you delegated to your assistant and what you had to correct.

    Choosing the problem

    Your question must name who decides what with your prediction: a ministry that targets a subsidy, a bank that approves a loan, a firm that tries to keep a customer. Two routes:

    • Your own dataset. Public data is fine (Kaggle, UCI, the World Bank, open government data, the Banco Central de Chile…), but the question must be yours. At least ~1,000 rows and a clear target.
    • CASEN, with another target. The course data has more questions in it than poverty: labour informality, public vs. private health insurance, overcrowding. Poverty and income are not allowed as targets, since we solve those in class.

    Either way, write down early which variables you would really have at prediction time. Most project disasters start there.

    Checkpoint 1: an honest first model

    Checkpoint 1 · 40% of the final grade

    Goal: a framed problem, a clean dataset and a first model whose error you can defend.

    Instructions

    1. Frame the problem. The question, the target, the decision that changes, and the cost of each kind of mistake.
    2. Prepare the data. One row per unit of analysis. Document every row you drop and every variable you transform, and what missing means for each column with missing values.
    3. Split first; baseline next. A trivial model (the mean, or the majority class) that every later result is measured against.
    4. Build a pipeline. Preprocessing inside a Pipeline, with linear or logistic regression and at least one KNN model, chosen by cross-validation.
    5. Evaluate with the right metric. For classification, a confusion matrix and a threshold you justify with the costs from step 1.
    6. Leakage check. One paragraph: which features did you remove or keep, and why each one would be available at prediction time.
    7. Assistant log (see Code Assistants).

    Grading rubric

    Criterion Weight
    Framing: the question, the target, the decision and the cost of each mistake 20%
    Data preparation, with documented choices (including missing values) 20%
    Pipeline and validation: split, baseline, CV, no leakage 30%
    Metric and threshold, read honestly 20%
    Clarity of the memo and the assistant log 10%

    Final delivery: compare, decide, defend

    Final delivery · 60% of the final grade

    Goal: take the Checkpoint 1 project to a recommendation, using the full toolbox of the course and the feedback you received.

    Instructions

    1. Respond to the feedback on Checkpoint 1: what you changed, and why.
    2. Apply at least three families of methods: your Checkpoint 1 model, a tree-based ensemble (Session 6), and either shrinkage (Session 7) or an unsupervised step (Session 8).
    3. Compare them honestly with cross-validation on the same splits, and explain the trade-offs: not only which one scored highest, but which one you would deploy and why.
    4. Interpret and recommend. What should the decision-maker do differently because of this model? Quantify the expected effect, with explicit assumptions.
    5. State the limitations, including what would break in production and what data you would need next.
    6. Assistant log.

    Grading rubric

    Criterion Weight
    Response to feedback and quality of the final pipeline 15%
    Breadth and correctness of the methods applied 25%
    Honest comparison and model choice 20%
    Recommendation, quantified and with limitations 20%
    Presentation and defence of your own code 20%

    A model you cannot explain is not a finished project

    The final presentation includes questions about your own code and the choices behind it. The assistant can write the pipeline, but only you can justify it.
